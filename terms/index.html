Terms

You bring the problem, we define the scope, build the solution, and agree the commercial terms around it. This page explains the basics of how that usually works.

Updated

March 5, 2026

Straightforward terms, written to match the tone of the rest of the site.

Scope

Project scope and proposals

Every project starts with a clear problem, a proposed scope, and a plan to deliver it.

The actual work is governed by the written proposal, scope, timeline, or agreement accepted for that engagement. That documentation defines what GRND Studio is building, whether the work covers strategy, custom software, AI implementation, automation, integrations, support, or phased delivery, and what sits outside the agreed scope.

Collaboration

Client responsibilities

Good delivery depends on access, feedback, and decisions arriving on time.

Clients are responsible for providing accurate project information, timely approvals, access to required systems, and any third-party credentials or materials needed to do the work. If feedback, access, or approvals are delayed, timelines and milestone planning can move with them.

Commercials

Payments, ownership, and support

Commercial terms are agreed per project, not guessed from the public site.

Payment schedules, intellectual property transfer, licensing, maintenance, and support commitments are defined in the applicable project agreement. Unless we state otherwise in writing, GRND Studio keeps ownership of pre-existing methods, reusable frameworks, internal tooling, and other background intellectual property used to deliver the work.

Governing

Liability and governing documents

This page gives the overview. Signed project terms still win.

A specific engagement may include additional terms covering confidentiality, data handling, warranties, acceptance, and allocation of risk. If a signed agreement exists, that agreement governs the relationship. If you want clarity on current terms for a proposed project, ask before work begins.
